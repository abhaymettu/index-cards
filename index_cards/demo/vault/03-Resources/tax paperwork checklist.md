# tax paperwork checklist

Running list so I stop scrambling in March. Check things off as they land through the year, not all at once.

## Income docs

- W-2 from employer, usually shows up in the portal by end of Jan. Download the PDF, don't trust the mailed copy
- 1099-INT / 1099-DIV from bank and brokerage. Brokerage ones often get *corrected* in Feb-March, so wait before filing
- 1099-B for any stock sales. Cross-check cost basis, it's wrong more often than I'd like
- RSU/ESPP: the W-2 already includes vest income but the broker's basis may not. Double-counting is easy here
- Any freelance or side payments: 1099-NEC if over threshold, but I still report it if under
- Equity statements (year-end supplement from the stock plan portal)

## Deductions / credits

- Mortgage interest statement (1098)
- Property tax: pull from county site, not just escrow statement
- Charity receipts. Anything over $250 needs written acknowledgment. Screenshot the email confirmations as I go
- HSA contributions (1099-SA and 5498-SA). Confirm I didn't blow past the limit with payroll + manual deposits
- 401k / IRA contributions. IRA contributions can still be made until the April deadline, so check the number before filing
- Home office: only if self-employed, W-2 folks can't deduct it. Don't bother measuring
- Student loan interest (1098-E)
- Health insurance form (1095-A if marketplace)

## Estimated payments

- Quarterly due dates: Apr 15, Jun 15, Sep 15, Jan 15. Put reminders two weeks ahead
- Safe harbor: pay 100% of last year's tax (110% if AGI over 150k) to avoid penalty
- Keep confirmation numbers in one note, not scattered in email
- Adjusting W-4 withholding mid-year is easier than making estimated payments

## Storage / filing

- Folder per year in the cloud drive: `taxes/2026/` with subfolders `income`, `deductions`, `returns`
- Name files `source_form_year.pdf` so they sort sensibly
- Keep returns and backup for at least 7 years. Keep basis records for held stock until well after selling
- Save a PDF of the final filed return plus the e-file acceptance email
- Last year's AGI is needed for e-file identity verification, so keep it handy

## Before hitting submit

- Names and SSNs match exactly what the SSA has
- Bank routing/account numbers for refund, read twice
- State return too, and check whether I moved or worked in another state
- Compare total income to last year; big swings need an explanation to myself first
