# reading list 2026

Goal this year: finish more than I start. Last year I abandoned about half of what I picked up, mostly because I bought books on vibes. Trying to keep the queue short and honest.

## How I'm tracking

- One queue, max 5 items "in progress" at a time. Anything past that goes in the backlog and stays there.
- Rule: give a book 50 pages. If I'm not pulled in, drop it, no guilt.
- Log a one-liner after finishing: what I'd steal from it, what I'd skip. Not a summary, just the takeaway.
- Ebook for commute stuff, paper for anything with diagrams or that I want to annotate.
- Library hold queue is way better than impulse buying. Holds take 2-3 weeks so plan ahead.

## Done so far (as of Nov)

- Distributed systems book, the long one. Slow going but worth it. Replication and consensus chapters finally clicked on the second pass. Skipped the formal proofs.
- Two books on incident response / postmortems. Overlapped a lot, only needed one. Take: blameless culture is mostly about how you phrase the timeline.
- A short one on writing clearly for engineers. Re-read twice. Changed how I write design docs (problem first, options second, recommendation last).
- A fiction novel in Q2 that I actually finished. Counts.

## In progress

- A book on negotiation. Reading it half for work, half for the next comp conversation.
- Long essay collection, one essay at a time before bed.

## Backlog (Q4 / 2027)

## Lessons

- Finished count matters less than retention. Writing the one-liner does more than rereading.
- Don't read technical books straight through. Skim the table of contents, jump to the chapter I need, circle back.
- Audiobooks fail for me on anything with code or diagrams. Fine for narrative nonfiction only.
- Aim for ~12 finished by Dec 31. Currently at 8, so a realistic push is needed in Nov/Dec.
