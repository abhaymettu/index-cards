# marathon training plan

16 weeks, 4 runs/wk + 1 cross-train. Building from ~25 km/wk base. Peak ~60 km.

## weekly shape

- Mon: rest or mobility (20 min, hips + calves)
- Tue: easy 8-10 km, conversational
- Wed: quality — intervals or tempo
- Thu: cross-train (bike or swim), easy
- Fri: rest
- Sat: easy 6 km + strides
- Sun: long run

Keep Tue/Sat truly easy. If I can't talk in full sentences, slow down. Most of my past blowups came from easy days being too fast.

## progression

- Weeks 1-4: base. Long run 14 → 18 km. Wed = 6x400 at 5k effort, full recovery.
- Weeks 5-8: long run 20 → 26 km, cutback in wk 8 (drop to ~18). Wed = tempo 20-30 min.
- Weeks 9-12: long run 28 → 32 km. Add marathon-pace blocks in the long run (3x5 km at goal pace). Wed = cruise intervals, 4x2 km.
- Weeks 13-14: peak, 32 km max, then start tapering.
- Weeks 15-16: taper. Volume down ~25% then ~40%. Keep a few short pace pickups so legs don't go flat.

Rule: no more than ~10% weekly mileage increase, and every 4th week is a cutback.

## fueling + gear

- Gel every 40 min on long runs past 90 min, ~60 g carbs/hr target. Practice it, don't improvise race day.
- Water at every aid station, electrolytes tab in the bottle on hot days.
- Race shoes: do at least 2 long runs and 1 tempo in them before race day.
- Replace daily trainers around 600 km. Check the tread, not just the odometer.
- Pre-long-run breakfast: oats + banana, 2-3 hrs before. Skip anything new.

## things to watch

- Shin or Achilles tightness lasting 2+ days → cut a run, don't push through.
- Sleep under 6 hrs two nights in a row → swap quality day for easy.
- Resting HR up 5+ bpm over baseline is a flag for fatigue or illness.
- Strength 2x/wk, short: single-leg deadlifts, calf raises, glute bridges, planks. Easy to skip, so tie it to after the Tue and Thu sessions.

## race day

- Start 10-15 sec/km slower than goal for the first 5 km.
- Fuel from km 8 on a schedule, not by feel.
- Don't try to bank time in the first half.
- Night before: lay out kit, pin bib, set two alarms.
