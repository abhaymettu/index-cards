# espresso dial-in notes

## baseline recipe
- 18g in, 36-38g out, 27-30s total. start here every time with a new bag
- water ~93C. drop to 91 if bitter on darker roasts, bump to 95 for light ones
- always weigh out, not just in. eyeballing the volume lies to me
- pre-infusion ~5s at low pressure helps with channeling, esp. on lighter roasts

## the fix-it table
- **sour / sharp / thin**: under-extracted. grind finer, or let yield run longer (40g out)
- **bitter / dry / ashy**: over-extracted. grind coarser, cut yield to 34-35g
- **hollow, salty**: way under. finer grind AND check dose
- **tastes fine but flat**: usually stale beans. past ~4 weeks off roast, just accept it
- change ONE variable at a time. grind first, then yield, then temp last

## grinder notes
- grinder steps are small near the fine end. a 1-notch change moves shot time by ~3-4s
- after changing grind, purge ~2g or the first shot is a lie (leftover grounds from old setting)
- fresh beans need coarser than I expect. as they age, go finer to keep the time
- clean burrs every couple weeks. rice trick works for a quick pass, but proper brush-out is better
- retention is annoying, so dose by weight into the portafilter after purge, not by hopper level

## puck prep
- WDT with a thin needle tool, every shot. fixes most of the spurting
- tamp level, firm, don't twist. pressure consistency matters less than levelness
- wet basket = bad. wipe it dry before dosing
- check the puck after pulling: wet/muddy with divots = channeling, dry and clean = good
- paper filter on the bottom smooths flow, worth it when testing a new grind

## troubleshooting
- shot runs under 20s even at fine setting: dose too low or beans too old
- choking the machine (nothing drips): way too fine, or overdosed. back off 2-3 notches
- inconsistent shot to shot: usually puck prep, not grind. check distribution first
- humidity swings change things. winter shots need slightly coarser than summer for me

## log template
bean / roast date / dose / grind / time / yield / taste (sour, bitter, balanced) / next change

Keep a running log in the daily note, otherwise I forget what the last setting was and start from zero again.
