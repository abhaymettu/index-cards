# espresso dial-in notes

## baseline recipe
- 18g in, 36-40g out, 27-32s total. Start here every time, change one thing at a time.
- water ~93C. Drop to 91-92 if the shot comes out harsh on darker roasts, bump to 94-95 for light roasts that taste sour/thin.
- basket is the 18g VST-style one, don't mix with the stock basket, numbers don't carry over.
- grinder setting is around 14 for medium roast. Light roast needs ~11-12. Dark ~16-17.

## reading the shot
- **sour, thin, salty-ish**: under-extracted. Grind finer first, then extend time/yield.
- **bitter, dry, ashy finish**: over-extracted. Grind coarser, or cut yield to ~34g.
- **hollow, flat but not sour**: usually too coarse + too long a ratio. Try finer and stop earlier.
- fast gush under 20s = too coarse or channeling. Check puck prep before touching the dial.
- if the first drops show up after 8s+ and flow is slow/sputtery, too fine or too much dose.

## puck prep
- WDT with a thin needle tool, 20 seconds, get the clumps at the bottom edge.
- level tamp, firm but not heroic. Consistency > force.
- wipe the basket rim before locking in, stray grounds cause side channeling.
- rinse the group head for 2s before loading, helps temp stability.

## beans / freshness
- rest 7-10 days after roast date for espresso. Day 3 shots are gassy and spritzy.
- peak window is roughly day 10-30. After ~6 weeks I have to go finer to compensate.
- store in the airtight canister, only grind what I'm dosing. No fridge.
- every new bag: expect to move the grind 1-2 steps from the last one, even same origin.

## workflow
- pull a sacrificial shot first if the machine's been idle for a while (cold grinder burrs retain old grounds ~1g).
- log: dose, yield, time, grind, temp, taste in one line so I can spot patterns.
- if I'm chasing a fix for more than 4 shots, stop and drink something else. Palate fatigue is real.
- milk drinks: go slightly finer/stronger (1:1.8 ratio) so it cuts through.
